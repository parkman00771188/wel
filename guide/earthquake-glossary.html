Key Terms

The vocabulary, in four groups

Thirty-six terms you will meet on this platform and in news coverage, grouped by what they describe.

By the World Earthquake Labs team · Checked against the USGS, ISC and JMA references listed at the end · Updated September 2026

The event

Epicenterthe point on the surface directly above where the rupture began
Hypocenter (focus)the point at depth where the rupture actually starts
Faulta fracture in the crust where blocks of rock move past each other
Rupturethe patch of fault that actually slipped, often hundreds of km long
Elastic reboundrock bends under strain, then snaps back — the basic mechanism
Aftershocksmaller quakes following the mainshock, decaying roughly as 1/time
Foreshocka quake preceding a larger one — only identifiable in hindsight
Swarmmany quakes of similar size with no clear mainshock, often volcanic

Waves and measurement

P wavethe fast compressional wave; arrives first, warns of what follows
S wavethe slower shear wave; carries most of the damaging motion
Surface wavesLove and Rayleigh waves, slowest and largest at distance
Moment magnitude (Mw)the standard scale; from rupture area × slip × rigidity
Seismic moment (M₀)the physical size of the rupture, in newton-metres
Intensity (MMI)shaking and damage at one place, graded I–XII
PGApeak ground acceleration, as a percentage of gravity
Seismometerthe instrument that records ground motion, in three directions
Seismogramthe recording itself — the trace P, S and surface waves leave
Saturationan older scale ceasing to grow once the quake exceeds its range
Focal mechanismthe "beachball" showing which way the fault moved
Gutenberg–Richter lawten times more quakes for each magnitude step down

Tectonic setting

Tectonic platea rigid slab of Earth's outer shell, moving cm per year
Subduction zonewhere one plate dives beneath another; home of every M 9
Wadati–Benioff zonethe sloping sheet of quakes inside a sinking slab
Transform faultplates sliding past each other, as at the San Andreas
Mid-ocean ridgewhere plates pull apart and new seafloor is made
Intraplate earthquakeone far from any boundary, like New Madrid
Induced seismicityquakes triggered by fluid injection, mining or reservoirs
Seismic gapa stretch of active fault that has not slipped in a long time

Effects and response

Liquefactionsaturated soil losing strength and behaving like a liquid
Lateral spreadingliquefied ground sliding downslope, tearing what sits on it
Tsunamiocean waves raised when a large undersea quake moves the seafloor
Run-uphow high above sea level a tsunami reaches on land
Earthquake early warningalerting from the P wave, seconds before the S wave
ShakeMapa map of observed and estimated shaking, made within minutes
Base isolationmounting a building on bearings so the ground moves, not it
Retrofitstrengthening an existing building to a later seismic standard
