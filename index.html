Research

Science that turns measurements into understanding.

Monitoring tells you what happened; research tells you what it means and what to expect next. Our scientists and engineers publish openly, release their code, and feed every improvement straight back into the operational platform — so a better algorithm on paper becomes a better earthquake solution in production.

Rapid Source Characterization

Waveform-inversion methods that estimate an earthquake's magnitude, rupture geometry, and mechanism within seconds of detection — the foundation for early warning and rapid impact assessment.

Seismic Hazard & Uncertainty

Global assessments of ground-shaking hazard that quantify not just the expected level of shaking, but how confident we can be in it — information engineers and insurers can actually build with.

Machine-Learned Detection

Deep-learning pickers and association models that find small earthquakes traditional pipelines miss, sharpening our picture of faults between the large events and improving catalog completeness.

Network Performance

Continuous evaluation of the global station network — noise levels, detection thresholds, and coverage gaps — published as technical reports that guide where the next instruments should go.

Open Data

High-quality seismic data, open to everyone.

Openness is not an afterthought — it is how earthquake science moves forward. Everything we produce is documented, versioned, and free to use for research, education, and operational applications alike.

At the core sits the Global Earthquake Catalog, a homogenized record of seismicity from 1900 to the present, reviewed by analysts and updated continuously as new solutions are published. Around it we maintain the supporting datasets that serious work requires:

  • Global Earthquake Catalog — origin times, hypocenters, magnitudes, and review status for every event, 1900–present.
  • Seismic Station Metadata — locations, instrument responses, and availability for 2,800+ stations, refreshed weekly.
  • Strong Motion Database — processed accelerograms and intensity measures from significant earthquakes.
  • Site & Fault Models — the global Vs30 grid and subduction-zone interface geometries used in hazard modeling.

Every dataset ships in open formats — CSV, JSON/GeoJSON, and QuakeML — with stable versioned releases you can cite, and the same REST API that powers this website.

# Query the public catalog API GET /v1/events?starttime=2026-08-01 &minmagnitude=4.5&format=geojson { "type": "FeatureCollection", "count": 312, "features": [ { "mag": 6.3, "place": "Off the coast of Chiapas", "depth": 22, "status": "reviewed" }, … ] }
