Live Map

Every earthquake since 1900 — on a map you can play.

The live map is two instruments in one. In 2D, a fast interactive world map for browsing recent seismicity; in 3D, the Earthquake 4D engine — a globe that replays the entire catalog through time and shows every hypocenter at its true depth.

Pick any window with the start–end date controls — the last 24 hours, a famous sequence from decades ago, or all 126 years at once — and both views follow. Press play and events light up in order; drag the time bar to scrub back and forth.

  • Any date range, 1900 → today. Presets from 24 hours to 10 years, or type exact dates.
  • 2D basemaps — topographic, satellite with night tint, ocean, gray, and streets — plus plate-boundary overlays.
  • The 4D globe renders depth in 3D: subducting slabs and deep seismic zones appear as real structures.
  • Layers & filters — magnitude bands, depth ranges, active faults, volcanoes, coloring by depth, magnitude, time, or density.

Earthquake Guide

Understand the shaking — then prepare for it.

A plain-language guide to earthquake science and safety, organized by topic so you can read the piece you need in a few minutes.

  • Basics — what an earthquake is, hypocenter vs epicenter, and how P and S waves make early warning possible.
  • Plate tectonics — convergent, divergent, and transform boundaries, and why 90% of quakes trace the Ring of Fire.
  • Magnitude vs intensity — the logarithmic scale, ×32 energy per step, and the M 2–9 class table with worldwide frequencies.
  • Key terms & FAQ — aftershocks, liquefaction, tsunamis; can quakes be predicted, and how alerts work.

The Safety Guide turns knowledge into habits: what to do indoors, outdoors, while driving, or in an elevator — each as a three-step drill — plus an interactive emergency-kit checklist that remembers your progress, and a three-point home readiness check.

Research

Science that turns measurements into understanding.

Our research program feeds every improvement straight back into the platform — a better algorithm on paper becomes a better earthquake solution in production.

Rapid Source Characterization

Waveform-inversion methods that estimate magnitude, rupture geometry, and mechanism within seconds of detection — the foundation for early warning.

Seismic Hazard & Uncertainty

Global assessments that quantify not just expected shaking but how confident we can be in it — numbers engineers and insurers can build with.

Machine-Learned Detection

Deep-learning pickers that find small earthquakes traditional pipelines miss, sharpening our picture of faults between the large events.

Network Performance

Continuous evaluation of the global station network — noise, thresholds, coverage — guiding where the next instruments should go.
