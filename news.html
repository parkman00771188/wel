About this feed

The headlines here are gathered automatically and link straight to the publishers; the reporting is theirs. This page adds the context that news reports usually cannot: the live catalogue the events come from, and the vocabulary to read the coverage well.

How the headlines are collected

Every ten minutes the collector reads the Google News RSS results for a rotating set of searches (earthquake, major earthquake damage, tsunami earthquake warning, magnitude 7 earthquake, aftershock sequence, earthquake early warning system, earthquake research study) together with ScienceDaily's earthquake feed. A headline must actually be about an earthquake to be kept; duplicates of the same story from different outlets are collapsed on the title; the newest 300 stay.

Each headline is sorted into one of three tabs by its wording. Network is anything about instruments and systems: seismometers, stations, early warning. Research is anything from a science outlet or that mentions a study, model or dataset. Everything else is an Event, a report on a particular earthquake.

The Earthquakes tab is different in kind: it is not news but the live USGS feed itself, the last 14 days of catalogued events, newest first. The Papers tab shows the newest journal articles the Research Hub collected.

Reading earthquake news well

The magnitude will change
The first figure is automatic and comes from the nearest stations. It is revised within hours, and for large events a moment magnitude replaces it. Headlines written in the first hour often carry a number the agencies have since moved by a few tenths.
Magnitude is not intensity
Magnitude is the size of the source; intensity is the shaking at a place. A deep M 7 can be felt gently over a whole country; a shallow M 6 under a city can be devastating. "How strong was it?" has two different answers.
Warning, advisory, watch
Tsunami messages come in grades. In the US system a warning means dangerous waves are expected, an advisory means strong currents and waves dangerous near the water, a watch means a threat is being evaluated. Japan's JMA grades are Major Tsunami Warning, Tsunami Warning and Tsunami Advisory. The grade matters more than the word "tsunami".
Aftershock forecasts are probabilities
After a large earthquake the USGS publishes the chance of further shocks of a given size over the coming days and weeks. That is a statistical forecast built on how sequences decay; it is not a prediction of a specific earthquake, and no agency issues those.
Go to the source
Every significant earthquake has an official event page with the current magnitude, depth, shaking maps and, where relevant, tsunami information. Find the event on the live map; its card links to the USGS page.
